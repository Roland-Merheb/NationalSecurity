================================================================================
1.  PURPOSE OF THIS PROPOSAL
================================================================================

This preliminary proposal presents the SHIELD concept and the equipment Strategic Defense offers to supply. It is a basis for technical discussion with the Office of the Special Adviser, not a final offer.

The final proposal will follow a site survey. It will confirm exact quantities, locations, and schedule.


================================================================================
2.  BACKGROUND AND OUR UNDERSTANDING
================================================================================

Nigeria's security challenge is less a lack of information than a failure to move it from collection to action in time. Agencies hold intelligence, but sharing is slow and field units often cannot act on it together.

The Office of the Special Adviser to the President on Homeland Security, created in May 2026, exists to close that gap through intelligence integration, inter-agency coordination, and proactive risk management. It builds on the Intelligence Fusion Centre established under the Office of the National Security Adviser, which already brings the major security agencies together in Abuja.

The next step is to extend that coordination to where threats occur: the land borders and the scene of a crisis. Two practical gaps stand out:

    a.  Unwatched borders. Long stretches of land border have no persistent
        surveillance, so illegal crossings go undetected.

    b.  Agencies that cannot talk. Police, military, and paramilitary units
        use different radio systems and cannot communicate directly at an
        incident.

SHIELD addresses both with equipment, delivered and working, not with further studies.

4.  PACKAGE A - BORDER SURVEILLANCE
================================================================================

Package A installs persistent surveillance on a pilot section of the South-West border with Benin, then expands to other sections once proven. The South-West is the most accessible land border for a first deployment, and its crossings are heavily used for smuggling.

The exact pilot section, for example along the main road corridors and the bush crossings between them, will be fixed during the site survey.

4.1  How it works

The system works in four layers:

    1.  Detect: ground radar and buried sensors pick up people and vehicles
        moving across the border, day and night.

    2.  Identify: cameras on the nearest tower turn automatically toward the
        alarm, so an operator can see what triggered it.

    3.  Report: video and alarms travel to the sector control room and on to
        national level.

    4.  Respond: the operator alerts the nearest patrol or border post, which
        moves to intercept.

4.2  Equipment

    Surveillance towers
        Masts carrying day/night and thermal cameras and ground surveillance
        radar, covering open and bush terrain.

    Unattended ground sensors
        Buried seismic and magnetic sensors along known crossing routes,
        raising an alarm when someone passes.

    Border post upgrades
        CCTV, vehicle barriers and gates, and space, power, and network ready
        for ID devices.

    Sector control room
        A room near the border with video display, operator consoles, and
        recording, where staff monitor towers and sensors.

    Tower power
        Solar panels with battery storage at every tower, with no dependence
        on the grid.

    Communications links
        Radio and satellite links carrying video and alarms from towers to
        the control room and onward.

    Mobile surveillance units
        Vehicles with a telescopic camera and radar mast, moved to where
        crossing patterns shift.

    Portable observation kits
        Man-portable tripod systems with thermal and day/night optics,
        carried by foot patrols to watch gaps between towers.

    Vehicle and cargo scanning
        X-ray scanners at border posts to inspect vehicles, cargo, and
        baggage for contraband without unloading.

    Perimeter protection
        Fencing, razor wire, and entry-point protection around border posts
        and the sector control room.

5.  PACKAGE B - JOINT RESPONSE AND INTEROPERABLE COMMUNICATIONS
================================================================================

Package B lets different agencies talk and coordinate as one team at the scene of a crisis, without replacing any agency's own radios.

5.1  How it works

When an incident occurs, such as an attack, a mass kidnapping, or unrest at a border crossing, the police, military, civil defence, and other units arrive on their own radio networks. The radio bridging equipment links those networks, so a commander can open a shared talk group across all agencies in minutes.

The mobile command vehicle becomes the on-scene command post. It carries the bridging equipment, a satellite link to national level, and a working space for the agency commanders. If mobile networks are down or cut, the emergency communication kits keep the scene connected.

5.2  Equipment

    Radio bridging equipment
        Gateways that interconnect different radio systems and frequency
        bands, fixed at command sites and portable for the field.

    Mobile command vehicle
        One fully equipped vehicle with radios, bridging equipment, satellite
        link, displays, and a commanders' meeting space.

    Emergency communication kits
        Satellite phones and portable satellite terminals for voice and data
        when terrestrial networks fail.

    Deployable command shelters
        Rapid-erect shelters with lighting and power distribution for longer
        operations or sites vehicles cannot reach.

    Standby power
        Generators and portable battery packs for the vehicle, shelters, and
        kits.

    Mobile relay masts
        Deployable masts with encrypted mesh radio, extending radio coverage
        into remote areas around an incident.

6.  DESIGN PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

All SHIELD equipment is chosen against six principles:

    a.  Technology-neutral: no brand is fixed in advance; equipment is
        selected against performance requirements.

    b.  Open standards: video, data, and radio interfaces use recognised
        standards, so SHIELD connects to national systems and avoids lock-in
        to a single supplier.

    c.  Modular and scalable: the pilot section and first vehicle form a
        template that can be copied to other border sections and zones.

    d.  Built for the environment: equipment is rated for heat, humidity,
        heavy rain, and dust.

    e.  Independent power: towers and field sites run on solar and batteries;
        command equipment carries its own backup.

    f.  Secure: links are encrypted, equipment is hardened against tampering
        and cyber attack, and access is controlled.
